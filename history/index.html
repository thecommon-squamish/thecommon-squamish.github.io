The story behind the space

Why The Common is The Common.

It began with desks. The point was always the people who would meet around them.

Built for more than a workday.

Peter Buchholz opened The Common on Cleveland Avenue in 2017. He wanted independent workers, local businesses, and people new to Squamish to have somewhere to focus and somewhere to find one another. An original 1970s building became a shared place for desks, conversation, lunch, and local gatherings.

The idea drew in part from Seats2meet in Utrecht: people bring experience, skills, time, and introductions to a place, not only the price of a desk. Peter called that social capital. It shaped what The Common tried next.

Moments that shaped the place.

These are chapters from The Common's original website, told as history. Older passes, grants, discounts, and event plans are not current offers.

September 2017

The door opened to Squamish.

The first open house invited people to work, look around, and stay for an evening speaker event. Futurist Nikolas Badminton was announced for a talk and Q&A about technology and the future of work. From the start, The Common was meant to host conversations as well as desks.

November 2017

Peter asked members to help build the community.

In his first update, Peter said he was proud of the space but had spent too little time connecting its founding members. He invited them to a Friday social, offered the first drink, asked for candid feedback, and promised more chances to gather. The note also tracked practical improvements members needed, from Wi-Fi and power to meeting space and the garden.

Read the first-weeks story
March 2018

Member travel inspired a pass across the Sea to Sky.

Founding members told the team they moved regularly between Squamish, Whistler, and Vancouver. The team worked with coworking spaces in Whistler and Vancouver to create a shared Common Pass. That former arrangement is no longer advertised as a current benefit; its lasting lesson is that the members shaped the offering.

How the pass came about
2018

Even local politics got a place at the table.

The Common announced an all-candidates air hockey tournament for the Squamish election, with proceeds intended for Howe Sound Women’s Shelter. The original post is an invitation, so we describe the plan rather than claiming a completed event or donation.

Over the years

Space and support for local groups.

Common Capital explored exchanging useful skills, time, and connections for more accessible desk space. With Community Futures, The Common described grants to help groups gather around entrepreneurship and innovation. Venue sponsorship aimed to make nonprofit events, fundraisers, and film nights easier to put on. These were historical programs; ask us what is possible today.

Explore the community programs
2021–2024

Still changing with the people who use it.

An April Fools announcement of outdoor offices at Newport Beach and the Stawamus Chief showed Peter's playful side. Real changes followed member needs: more call options, better desks and monitors, a refreshed Micro Office, and more room for the lounge and shared work. Specific layouts and equipment have continued to change, so the current room pages show what you can book now.

What that looks like today.

People still arrive to get their own work done. The shared tables, kitchen, courtyard, and Thursday community lunch make it easier to meet someone along the way. The next chapter belongs to the people who work, meet, and build things here now.
