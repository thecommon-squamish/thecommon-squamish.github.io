Why Peter opened The Common.

Peter Buchholz opened The Common in an original 1970s building on Cleveland Avenue. He knew the pull of working remotely, and the part it could leave out: a conversation over lunch, someone to ask for a second opinion, a reason to leave the house.

He made room for both sides of the day. There are desks where you can focus, and a kitchen, communal tables, and a courtyard where you can get to know the people sharing the space.

In his first update to members, Peter admitted that building the room was only the beginning. He wanted to spend more time bringing the people in it together. That thought still guides The Common.

The first weeks at The Common

Squamish Après gathering photo: Oisin McHugh.

Come in, get settled, get something done.

Take a desk by the front windows or tuck into the main room. Make your lunch in the kitchen. Step outside for a break, then come back to the thing you were working on.

Some people come in for an hour. Some are here most days. Either way, you don't have to work alone.

Try a day here

A place to work, and people to work alongside.

Coders, lawyers, students, engineers, green tech founders, local business owners, and people working remotely have all found their way here.

The work is different. The lunch table, the garden, and the conversations between meetings are shared. That's what keeps The Common feeling like Squamish.

How The Common began

Some things we've done along the way.

2017

An open house with a talk.

The first open house invited Squamish to work, look around, and stay for a planned talk by futurist Nikolas Badminton.

2018

A pass for people on the move.

Early members told the team they travelled between Squamish, Whistler, and Vancouver. The historical Sea to Sky Common Pass grew out of those conversations.

2018

Politics, meet air hockey.

For the local election, The Common announced an all candidates air hockey tournament, with proceeds intended for Howe Sound Women’s Shelter.

2021

A little April Fools mischief.

The Common announced two outdoor offices: one at Newport Beach and one on the Stawamus Chief. It was an April Fools joke, and a very Squamish one.
